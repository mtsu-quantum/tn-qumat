### Overview {#overview .scroll-mt-32}

![AMPLIFY Scholars 2026](/images/news/amplify-2026/amp_title.jpg)

**AMPLIFY is a competitive, paid 10-week summer research internship for undergraduate STEM students** with an interest in quantum materials, computation, experiments, and materials science. Funded by the U.S. Department of Energy RENEW program through the TN-QuMat-ORNL consortium, it brings together Middle Tennessee State University (MTSU), Tennessee State University (TSU), Fisk University, Meharry Medical College, and Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL) to build a quantum-ready workforce in Tennessee.

Each scholar is matched with a faculty mentor to conduct real quantum research. The program is built around whole-scholar development, combining research with weekly mentoring and professional development to prepare students for graduate school, national fellowships, and careers in the quantum workforce.

AMPLIFY is designed for students who are curious, motivated, and ready to take a lead role in their research, regardless of how much prior lab experience they bring. By the end of 10 weeks, scholars will have conducted real research, built a scientific poster, learned to read and discuss primary literature, and have a clear, supported path toward graduate school and the quantum workforce.

### What Scholars Receive

- **Paid internship:** $3,500 stipend for 10 weeks, at $17.50 per hour for 20 hours per week
- **Mentored research:** faculty-mentored research in quantum materials, using high-performance computing and experimental research facilities, including ORNL's world-class computing and TSU's experimental facilities
- **One-on-one mentorship:** weekly mentoring from TN-QuMat faculty and scientists
- **Paper reading:** journal clubs, primary literature workshops, and scientific writing training
- **Poster and presentation training:** guided poster design, rehearsal sessions, and presentation support for MTSU Scholars Week and national conferences
- **Graduate school preparation:** coaching on Ph.D. applications, personal statements, program selection, and interviews
- **Fellowship support:** guided applications for NSF REU, DOE SULI at ORNL, the NSF Graduate Research Fellowship (GRFP), and the Goldwater Scholarship
- **Professional development:** regular workshops and cohort meetings with scholars from across the partnership

### Research Tracks {#topics .scroll-mt-32}

**A. Modeling and Simulations of Quantum Systems.** Theoretical and computational study of strongly correlated electrons, disorder effects, and quantum phase transitions using dynamical mean-field theory and quantum Monte Carlo.

**B. First-Principles Simulations of Quantum Materials.** Density functional theory and ab initio methods applied to topological insulators, Weyl semimetals, and materials with protected surface states for quantum information science.

**C. Synthesis and Characterization of Low-Dimensional Quantum Structures; Quantum Optics.** Laboratory synthesis, thin-film growth, and spectroscopy of 2D materials and novel superconductors using ORNL neutron scattering facilities, and quantum optics.

### Who Should Apply

- Undergraduate students from all STEM majors, including Physics, Chemistry, Computer Science, Mathematics, Biology, Engineering, Data Science, and more
- Any year, freshman through senior, with no prior quantum research required
- Students enrolled at MTSU, TSU, Fisk University, or Meharry Medical College

### How to Apply

The online application takes about 15 to 25 minutes. Applicants are asked about their academic background, research interests, motivation, and career goals, and they upload unofficial transcripts. Applicants select the research thrusts that interest them and are then matched with a faculty mentor.
